# Diagnostic axes

Load when: diagnosing in any mode. Pick the axes the request and lenses call for; say which you skipped.

Each axis lists questions for `plan` mode (outline, framework, treatment) and `text` mode (actual pages), the usual flags, and the lenses to pull. Questions are prompts for your reading, not a form to fill in for the author. A flag is a hypothesis until you have evidence.

## 1. Intent and design

- **Plan:** Can the designing principle (how the story unfolds, not what happens) be said in one sentence? Does every unit serve it, or are some units there because the material was available? Is the intent in the profile visible in the plan, or only in the author's head?
- **Text:** Where does the text carry out the design without commentary? Where does the narrator or a character explain the design instead of enacting it?
- **Flags:** `DESIGN_IN_HEAD_ONLY`: the plan's structure doesn't express the stated intent; the intent lives in notes. `STATED_NOT_ENACTED`: the meaning is announced in dialogue or narration rather than produced by events.
- **Lenses:** Truby's designing principle, Egri's premise as a test, Vygotsky (form vs. material).

## 2. Shape and proportion

- **Plan:** What macro shape is this actually (see `shapes.md`)? What shape does the author intend? How is the length distributed: which units are heavy, which are thin, and does the weight fall where the meaning falls? If units carry formal names (acts, movements, parts of a dance), does each perform its slot's function?
- **Text:** Do unit lengths and densities match their importance? Where does the shape break (a spiral that stops tightening, a frame that stops returning, a refrain that stops changing)?
- **Flags:** `SHAPE_MISMATCH`: actual shape differs from the intended one. `TITLE_AS_WALLPAPER`: unit names promise a form the units don't perform. `MISPLACED_WEIGHT`: the most space goes to what matters least.
- **Lenses:** `shapes.md`, Alison, M. Chekhov's composition, jo-ha-kyū.

## 3. Event and turn

- **Plan:** In each unit, what changes that cannot be undone? What boundary is crossed (from which state, world or role into which)? Which units contain no event, only situation, and is that deliberate (stillness) or empty (stasis)?
- **Text:** Does each scene turn a value or situation from one charge to another? Where is the gap between what a character expects and what happens? Is the event on the page or reported? If reported, why?
- **Flags:** `NON_EVENT`: the unit ends where it began. `ITERATED_NON_EVENT`: a recurring event that the reader can predict and that is not renewed each time, so it stops counting as an event. `OFFSTAGE_EVENT`: the key change happens between scenes and is only mentioned.
- **Lenses:** Lotman and Schmid's eventfulness, McKee's value turn and gap, Knebel/Tovstonogov event series, Aristotle's reversal and recognition.

## 4. Causality and necessity

- **Plan:** Between consecutive units, is the link "therefore" or "but", or only "and then"? If one unit were removed, what would break? Which turns depend on coincidence, and how many coincidences does the work spend? Where coincidence helps the protagonist, is that earned?
- **Text:** Are motivations on the page before the actions that need them? Where does a character act because the plot needs it rather than because of who they are?
- **Flags:** `AND_THEN_CHAIN`: units connected only by sequence. `REMOVABLE_UNIT`: a unit whose removal changes nothing (Aristotle's episodic plot). `CONVENIENT_COINCIDENCE`: chance resolves what choice should. `MOTIVATION_AFTER_THE_FACT`: the reason arrives only after the act.
- **Lenses:** Aristotle on probability and necessity, Tomashevsky's motivation, Forster's story vs. plot.

## 5. Agency and choice

- **Plan:** At each turning point, who chooses? Do choices come from characters, or from fate, chance, a system or the author? Does any character make an interesting wrong choice?
- **Text:** Where is a character a puppet (acts without inner cause, or is moved by the narration)? Where does passive construction ("it happened", "mistakes were made") take away a choice that the work needs?
- **Flags:** `DYSFUNCTIONAL_NARRATIVE`: nobody acts or takes responsibility; things happen to everyone. `PUPPET_MOMENT`: an action required by the plot and unmotivated by the character. `FATE_AS_ALIBI`: a deterministic frame (destiny, karma, system) removes choices that the drama still needs. Diagnose the execution, not the frame.
- **Lenses:** Baxter's dysfunctional narratives, Stanislavsky's task and through-line, Truby's moral argument.

## 6. Character

- **Plan:** For each major character: what they want, what they lack or fear, the role or function they serve in the structure, and how they change or deliberately don't. Is any character only a function (a slot the plot needs)? Do mirror or double characters differ meaningfully?
- **Text:** Do characters contradict themselves in ways that reveal rather than confuse? Do two characters sound alike? Does each voice hold its own truth, or does the narrator judge them?
- **Flags:** `FUNCTION_ONLY`: a person reduced to a plot role. `VOICE_BLUR`: distinct characters indistinguishable in speech or perception. `MONOLOGIC_VERDICT`: the narration settles who is right, flattening a conflict that should stay open. `UNEARNED_CHANGE` / `UNEXPLAINED_STASIS`: a change without pressure, or no change where the design requires one.
- **Lenses:** Propp (functions vs. performers), Bakhtin's polyphony, Forster's flat and round characters, Weiland's arcs (only if declared).

## 7. Stakes and pressure

- **Plan:** What can be lost in each unit, and by whom? Does the frame itself (narrator's present, framing situation) have stakes, or is it only a delivery device? Do stakes rise, shift or deepen, and is a flat line deliberate?
- **Text:** Does the reader feel the cost, or only hear about it? Where is the pressure on a character at its highest, and is that also where the text spends the most attention?
- **Flags:** `STAKES_FREE_FRAME`: the frame carries nothing to win or lose. `ABSTRACT_STAKES`: stakes named (the world, the soul) but not embodied in anything concrete. `PRESSURE_RELEASED_EARLY`: tension discharged before the event that needs it.
- **Lenses:** McKee's gap, Stanislavsky's предлагаемые обстоятельства, Brooks (the end shaping the middle).

## 8. Information economy

- **Plan:** Build the information ledger: for each key fact, when the reader learns it and when each character learns it. Where is the reader ahead (irony, suspense), level, or behind (mystery, surprise)? Which reveal is the work's main one, and what has prepared it?
- **Text:** Does the reader always know who, where and when, or is disorientation deliberate and productive? Is any reveal unfair (withheld only by authorial trickery) or cheap (announced too early)? Are there pages where the reader knows more than needed and tension drains away?
- **Flags:** `ORIENTATION_FAILURE`: the reader can't tell who, where or when, and it isn't productive. `UNFAIR_REVEAL`: the surprise was impossible to anticipate, even in retrospect. `REVEAL_SPENT_EARLY`: the main revelation discharged before it can work. `SUSPENSE_LEAK`: the reader is told what should have been felt as threat.
- **Lenses:** Hitchcock's suspense vs. surprise (`stage-and-screen.md`), Shklovsky's retardation, Brooks's anticipation of retrospection.

## 9. Motif and image system

- **Plan:** List the recurring motifs, objects, images and phrases. For each: is it bound (removing it breaks the causal chain) or free (it serves the telling)? Does it change meaning across its appearances? Do set-ups have pay-offs, and do pay-offs have set-ups?
- **Text:** Does each recurrence vary (accrue meaning) or merely repeat? Which motifs are accidental (an unplanned repetition of a word or image the reader will read as meaningful)? Where is a motif explained by the narrator instead of trusted?
- **Flags:** `DECORATIVE_MOTIF`: present, never changing, carrying nothing. `ORPHAN_SETUP`: planted and never paid off. `UNPLANTED_PAYOFF`: a payoff with nothing before it. `ACCIDENTAL_MOTIF`: unplanned repetition that the reader will read as a signal. `EXPLAINED_SYMBOL`: the text tells the reader what the image means.
- **Lenses:** Tomashevsky, Truby's symbol web, M. Chekhov on repetition, Baxter's rhyming action, Chekhov's gun (applied as motif economy, not literally).

## 10. Theme and moral argument

- **Plan:** What question does the work test? Is it argued through choices and consequences, or stated? Does the strongest opposing position get a real voice?
- **Text:** Is there a mouthpiece character who speaks the thesis? Is the ending a verdict the narration hands down, or a result the action earns? If the work refuses an answer, is the refusal felt as tension or as evasion?
- **Flags:** `MOUTHPIECE`: a character exists to deliver the author's position. `STRAW_OPPONENT`: the opposing view is set up to lose. `THESIS_ENDING`: the ending illustrates a conclusion instead of resolving the action. `EVASIVE_AMBIGUITY`: open meaning that comes from not deciding, not from deliberate tension.
- **Lenses:** Truby's moral argument, Bakhtin's polyphony, Egri's premise as a test, Hegel's collision of justified powers (`lenses-classical.md`).

## 11. Time, narration and frame

- **Plan:** Build the fabula/syuzhet table. Why is each displacement where it is? Who tells, from when, to whom, and why now? How does the frame interact with what it frames: does the inner story change the frame's situation, or the frame change how we read the inner story?
- **Text:** Is the narrative distance stable, or does it shift with purpose? Is the point of view consistent with what the narrator could know? What is crowded (dwelt on) and what is leapt over, and does that pattern serve the work?
- **Flags:** `ARBITRARY_DISPLACEMENT`: a time jump with no effect. `POV_LEAK`: the narrator knows what they can't. `INERT_FRAME`: the frame doesn't change and isn't changed. `RETURN_AS_PUNCTUATION`: returning to the frame used only to end units, with no new information.
- **Lenses:** Shklovsky's fabula/syuzhet, Brooks, Le Guin's crowding and leaping, Bakhtin's chronotope, `shapes.md` (frame, nested frames, analytic drama).

## 12. Tempo and rhythm

- **Plan:** Where does the pace accelerate and where does it slow, at the scale of the whole and inside each unit? Is there a gradient (slow opening, gathering, quick close) or a deliberate refusal of one?
- **Text:** What is the scene-to-summary ratio, and does it shift where it should? Does slowness have presence (stillness) or is it empty (stasis)? Where does a delay build tension and where does it stall?
- **Flags:** `STASIS_NOT_STILLNESS`: slow stretches with nothing under tension. `FLAT_TEMPO`: the same pace throughout, whatever the content. `RETARDATION_STALL`: delay that only postpones.
- **Lenses:** jo-ha-kyū, Shklovsky's retardation, Baxter on stillness, Swain's scene and sequel (pacing inside a unit only).

## 13. Openings and endings

- **Plan:** What does the opening promise (tone, question, mode of reading)? Does the ending answer the promise the work actually made, not a conventional one? Are the opening and ending in tension (polarity), so the movement between them means something?
- **Text:** Does the first unit teach the reader how to read the book? Is the ending earned by what precedes it, or attached? If the work is cyclical, does the return show difference, so it is not merely a trip around the block?
- **Flags:** `BROKEN_PROMISE`: the opening sets up a mode or question the work abandons. `UNEARNED_ENDING`: resolution arrives without the action producing it. `CIRCLE_WITHOUT_DIFFERENCE`: the ending returns to the opening and nothing has changed in meaning.
- **Lenses:** M. Chekhov's polarity, Brooks, Baxter's rhyming action, Aristotle's whole.

## 14. World and rules

- **Plan:** What are the world's rules (physical, social, metaphysical)? Do they cost the characters something? Are they consistent across units? Does the critique understand the metaphysics before judging it?
- **Text:** Where is a rule explained in exposition instead of shown in consequences? Where does a rule bend for plot convenience?
- **Flags:** `RULE_BENDS_FOR_PLOT`: the world's logic breaks exactly when the plot needs it to. `EXPOSITION_DUMP`: rules delivered as lecture. `COSTLESS_RULE`: a rule that never constrains anyone.
- **Lenses:** Truby's story world, Bakhtin's chronotope.

## 15. Reader experience

- **Plan:** At each point, what question is the reader carrying forward? Is there always at least one open question pulling them on? Where would a first-time reader stop caring?
- **Text:** Map orientation, confusion, prediction and emotional response moment by moment (the panel's cold reader does this best). Is confusion productive (a question the reader wants answered) or a failure (they can't follow)?
- **Flags:** `NO_OPEN_QUESTION`: nothing pulls the reader forward. `PRODUCTIVE_VS_DEAD_CONFUSION`: classify every confusion point, don't just count it.
- **Lenses:** `reader-panel.md`, Gardner's continuous dream (as a risk check, not a law), Brooks.
